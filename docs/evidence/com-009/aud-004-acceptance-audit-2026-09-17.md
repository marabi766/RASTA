## 5. The blocker, and the smallest next task

**Blocker.** identity-service answers a caller with a valid internal service token with `403 FORBIDDEN`
(`packages/nest-common/src/guards/auth.guard.ts`, "This endpoint is not callable by another service"), and that refusal
is not recorded:

- this is reachable today — identity has no `@AllowService` endpoint;
- ADR-053 § 4 names `FORBIDDEN` in the MVP refusal scope;
- plan § 5 row 1 requires a `REFUSED` record for a `403` on the instrumented service;
- no source places it out of scope.

**Smallest next engineering task — AUD-004 Phase C11:** record the auth guard's service-caller `403 FORBIDDEN` in
identity-service. Reuse the existing capture, aggregation, outbox, relay and contract.

- **Failing-test target:** `services/identity-service/test/security-event-auth-guard.int-spec.ts`, today's case "captures
  nothing for … a service caller". The service-caller probe must produce exactly one aggregated `REFUSED` row:
  - `actorType = SERVICE`, `actorId` = the calling service;
  - `errorCode = FORBIDDEN`;
  - a response byte-identical to today's;
  - plus a failed-capture case that still returns the identical `403`.
- **Additional proof:** a Kafka assertion in `security-event-kafka.int-spec.ts`.
- **Design point to settle first, in `docs/24`:** the tenant attribution of a service caller — the token's organization
  context, or a platform (`NULL`) row, which the outbox already supports.

**Alternative that avoids the engineering task:** the product owner records a dated decision that service-token refusals
belong with R-2. Rows 1a, 5 and 11 then become OOS, and the verdict becomes (B).
